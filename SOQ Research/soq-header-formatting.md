# Formatting the Header of a California State SOQ: Should You Add Your Name and a "Statement of Qualifications" Title When Not Asked?

## TL;DR
- **Yes — add your name (plus the job control number) and a "Statement of Qualifications" title at the top, even when the posting gives no header instructions.** This is exactly what California's own official guidance recommends: CalHR's "How to Write a Statement of Qualifications" states, "Unless directed otherwise, include your name and the job control number at the top of each page of your SOQ," and the state's own SOQ templates literally pre-label "Write Your Name Here" and "Statement of Qualifications."
- **Doing so is not risky or penalized in any source reviewed** — official, departmental, or practitioner. The only real risk is the opposite: ignoring formatting instructions that *are* given, since adherence to instructions is itself scored and failure can disqualify you.
- **Keep the header compact** because it sits inside the page limit (typically two pages total). Use a one-line header rather than a full business-letter block so you preserve space for the substantive, scored answers.

## Key Findings

**1. Official CalHR guidance explicitly endorses a name + JC-number header (OFFICIAL GUIDANCE).** The California Department of Human Resources (CalHR) document "How to Write a Statement of Qualifications" (Revised 3/26/2024) instructs, under its "Write" section, verbatim: "Unless directed otherwise, include your name and the job control number at the top of each page of your SOQ." This is the single most authoritative answer to the core question: in the absence of contrary instructions, a name/JC header is the recommended default, not a gray area.

**2. The State's own SOQ templates include both a name line and a "Statement of Qualifications" title (OFFICIAL TEMPLATE).** The California Department of Justice / Office of the Attorney General publishes an SOQ template whose top reads, verbatim: "Write Your Name Here / Statement of Qualifications – C.E.A. Exam / Statement of Qualifications – (ENTER POSITION TITLE HERE)." It directs applicants to put their name and the document title at the top — confirming that a title is expected, not presumptuous.

**3. Many individual departments affirmatively *require* a name/JC/title header (DEPARTMENT-SPECIFIC).** Several postings instruct applicants to put their name, job control number, and the document title in the header. A Caltrans (California Department of Transportation) Federal Planning Manager posting stated verbatim: "In addition to the above instructions, you must include 'Statement of Qualifications', your name, and the job control number centered and in bold in the header. Failure to include and complete the SOQ may result in disqualification for interview." A Department of Health Care Services posting instructed applicants to indicate their full name and job control number at the top right corner. These show that when departments speak to headers at all, they want exactly the elements in question.

**4. Following stated formatting/instructions is itself scored — and failure can disqualify you (OFFICIAL GUIDANCE).** CalHR's HR Manual section 3004 states, verbatim: "The applicant is required to follow all instructions, as failure to do so can result in disqualification from the examination process." The OAG template warns, verbatim: "Please carefully read the instructions as you will be rated on your ability to adhere to the filing requirements, in addition to how well your experience and/or education meets the Desirable Qualifications (DQs) of the position." CalHR's candidate guidance adds: "Not following the SOQ instructions may disqualify you." So the asymmetry is clear: adding a sensible header when none is specified is safe; ignoring a header/format that IS specified is dangerous.

**5. Typical formatting: two pages, 12-point font, single- or double-spaced — and the header counts toward the limit (OFFICIAL/DEPARTMENTAL).** The most common spec is "no more than two pages, single-spaced, 12-point font." The OAG template states verbatim: "Be sure to closely follow the formatting requirements: 12-point font, single-spaced, no more than two pages total... your SOQ cannot be more than two pages total in length." Some postings vary — one CalCareers posting required 12-point Arial, double-spaced; CDCR exempt postings allow up to four pages. Because limits are stated as "two pages total in length," any header you add sits within the counted pages, which is the practical reason to keep it to one compact line.

## Details

**The authoritative chain of guidance.** California's SOQ ecosystem has three tiers, and they agree:

- *CalHR (statewide policy):* The HR Manual section 3004 defines the SOQ as "a selection instrument" that "also provides a sample of the applicant's writing skills" and is often "scored as part of the application screening process." CalHR's separate candidate-facing tip sheet ("How to Write a Statement of Qualifications," rev. 3/26/2024) gives the operative header rule — name + job control number at the top of each page unless directed otherwise — and the operative warning — follow font/size/length instructions or risk disqualification.
- *DGS Office of Human Resources (statewide-facing):* Its "How to Write a Statement of Qualifications" page tells applicants to "Ensure you follow all SOQ instructions regarding font, font size and number of pages," and notes grammar, clarity, and legibility "are considered in the evaluation process."
- *Individual departments (CDCR, Caltrans, DHCS, FI$Cal, CDFA, DOJ/OAG):* These echo the same points and frequently publish their own templates or header requirements. CDFA's SOQ fact sheet stresses "follow all directions for the SOQ, including font, margin, and page requirements." DOJ's "How to apply" page warns that "generic" SOQs "are usually scored lower for not adhering to the SOQ prompt" and that "most SOQs are scored using a standard rubric."

**Why a header helps rather than hurts.** Reviewers may read hundreds of SOQs, and a submitted SOQ is often physically separated from the STD 678 application and resume in the screening packet — CalHR explicitly cautions, "do not assume your reader has reviewed your resume as there may be different reviewers for each portion of your application." A name + JC header is the simplest way to ensure your document is correctly attributed if pages are printed or separated. That is precisely the logic behind CalHR's "top of each page" instruction.

**Practitioner and career-coach consensus (GENERAL BEST PRACTICE / PRACTITIONER).** California Job Blog advises that if no specific formatting instructions are given, default to "Helvetica or Arial 12-point font with normal margins," and warns that many bulletins say "the screener will stop reading at a certain point." A named California DOJ recruiter (Jared Cottini, Workforce, Analytics and Recruitment Unit), writing on LinkedIn, frames the SOQ opening as a self-introduction and stresses "Try not to exceed the page limit" and "Don't restate your cover letter." General career sites (Resume Genius, Monster) recommend a professional header with "your name, contact information, the position title you're applying for, and its job reference number." No source — official or practitioner — describes adding a name/title header as risky or penalized.

**Nuance and disagreement.** The disagreements among sources are about *degree*, not *direction*:
- *How elaborate the header should be.* Non-California career blogs (Resume Genius, Monster) recommend a full business-letter block (your address, date, agency address, salutation). That is general private-sector/cover-letter convention and is heavier than CA state practice needs. California's own guidance asks only for name + job control number — a single compact line. For CalCareers SOQs, the lean CA-specific version is preferable because the block counts against a tight page limit.
- *Title wording.* The state template uses the literal title "Statement of Qualifications" (often followed by the position title). That is the safe, conventional label.
- *Whether to weave identity into a paragraph vs. a header.* Some practitioners open with an introductory paragraph naming their current title/agency rather than a formal header. This is stylistic; it does not conflict with also placing a name/JC line at the top.

The genuinely CA-state-specific conventions are: (a) the "name + job control number at the top of each page" rule, (b) the "Statement of Qualifications" title label, and (c) the centrality of scored adherence to stated formatting. The general-best-practice layer (1-inch margins, 12-pt Arial, single-spacing, tailoring to the posting) overlaps with CA practice but is not unique to it.

## Recommendations

**Default (no header instructions given):**
1. Add a single compact header line at the top: your full name and the job control (JC) number — exactly as CalHR recommends. If your SOQ runs to a second page, repeat it (CalHR says "top of each page").
2. Add a short title beneath or beside it: "Statement of Qualifications" — optionally followed by the position/classification title, mirroring the state template. This is expected, not presumptuous.
3. Keep the header to one or two lines (or place it in the Word "header" margin area) so it doesn't consume the body space the scorers actually read. Use 12-point Arial, single-spaced, 1-inch margins as a safe default when none is specified.

**When the posting specifies formatting (font, size, spacing, margins, page limit, header format, how to label/number answers):**
1. Follow those instructions exactly — adherence is itself scored and deviation can disqualify you.
2. If the posting prescribes a header format (e.g., Caltrans's "centered and in bold," or DHCS's "top right corner"), use that exact format instead of your default.
3. Number or restate each question/desirable qualification as instructed; address every part of multi-part questions.

**Benchmarks that would change the approach:**
- *If the posting says the screener stops reading at the page limit or that content beyond X pages "will not be scored,"* tighten the header to the absolute minimum (single line) and devote all remaining space to substantive answers.
- *If the posting provides a fillable SOQ template,* use the template as-is and do not add a competing header.
- *If the posting explicitly says to omit identifying information* (rare, used for blind scoring), follow that — this is the "unless directed otherwise" exception in CalHR's rule.

## Caveats
- **Instructions always win.** The "add name + title" recommendation is the *default for silence*. Any explicit instruction (including one to omit a header) overrides it.
- **Page limits are real and the header is inside them.** Because limits are stated as total pages "in length," a bulky letterhead can cost you scored space; keep it minimal.
- **Postings vary.** Font (Arial vs. unspecified), spacing (single vs. double), and length (2 vs. 4 pages) differ by department and even by posting, so re-read each bulletin's "Special Requirements"/SOQ section every time.
- **Reddit/r/CAStateWorkers practitioner quotes could not be independently retrieved** through available search tools; the practitioner layer here rests on California Job Blog (and its commenters), a named CA DOJ recruiter on LinkedIn, and general career sites. This does not affect the official-guidance conclusions, which are well documented.
- **No source found** treats adding a name or "Statement of Qualifications" title as risky or grounds for a lower score; the documented scoring risks all concern failing to follow instructions that were given, generic/non-responsive content, and poor grammar/clarity.
