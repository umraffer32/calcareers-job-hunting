## How SOQs are actually scored (the machinery)

1. **Rating criteria are written BEFORE the posting goes live** and must sort applicants into exactly three tiers: Not Qualified / Qualified / Well Qualified. The hiring manager (not central HR) writes and applies them. HR only verifies MQs and list eligibility. Sources: CalHR HR Manual 3004 (hrmanual.calhr.ca.gov/Home/ManualItem/1/3004), CDFA Best Hiring Practices (cdfa.ca.gov/FairsAndExpositions/Documents/2020BestHiringPractices.pdf).
2. **Scoring is per-factor and numerical against a written matrix**, kept 5 years for merit-challenge defense. CalHR publishes a sample matrix (calhr.ca.gov/Documents/labor-relations-hiring-process.pdf, p.4). Its rows include: eligibility, "Application completed correctly, displays attention to detail, neatness," **"Currently performing the same work,"** intern/student experience doing the same work, education in the field, then job-specific KSAs.
3. **The SOQ is typically THE instrument that decides interviews** ("an applicant's performance in the SOQ will serve as a basis for movement to the next stage"). CDFA caps its interview pool at "top candidates (up to a maximum of 10)".
4. **Format violations are disqualification grounds, not style points.** "Failure to provide an SOQ and follow instructions can result in the applicant being disqualified" (CalHR 3004). Confirmed in current posting boilerplate.
5. **Writing mechanics are themselves scored.** "Grammar, clarity of expression, and legibility are considered in the evaluation process" appears verbatim in CDCR, DGS, and SCO/CalHR guidance.
6. **Official guidance assumes the rater is skimming hundreds.** CalHR/SCO candidate guidance (rev. 3/2024): "Assume reviewers are reading hundreds of SOQs and make use of clear topic sentences, paragraph breaks and subheadings or bullet points." (sco.ca.gov/Files-EO/hr_statement_of_qualification.pdf)
7. **The SOQ must stand alone.** "Do not assume your reader has reviewed your resume as there may be different reviewers for each portion of your application."
8. **A generic SOQ is an elimination event.** Caltrans D4: "submitting a general SOQ will remove you from the competition."
9. **Top band = per-factor evidence with where/when/what/how/why plus an outcome**, duty-statement vocabulary, each desirable qualification addressed individually (CDCR) with roughly equal space (insider: cajobblog.com). Official mistake lists: recycled SOQs, typos, unexplained acronyms/jargon, passive voice, vague claims without examples, **not answering every part of multi-part questions**.
10. **An SOQ requirement is a competition signal**: CalHR says SOQs "may not be appropriate" for hard-to-fill/low-applicant postings. Where a posting skips the SOQ, the pool is typically thinner.

## STD 678 findings

11. **SCO's Work4CA checklist claims 1,500 characters per Duties Performed field, but this is WRONG in practice.** Tested and confirmed (July 2026): the field cuts off past ~1,000 characters. The house ~1,000-char budget stands. Do not trust the SCO figure; do not re-test.
12. **Zero credit for unlisted duties.** "If you don't list it, the hiring manager cannot assume, based on your classification or job title, what duties you performed."
13. **Tailoring the 678 per application is officially recommended** (re-order duties, swap verbs to match the duty statement, delete irrelevant experience; CalCareers supports 10 saved templates). CDT: "It's okay to use verbs directly from the job posting and duty statement; you may use them as much as possible."
14. **The 678 is read at the comparative stage too**, not just MQ. Neatness/completeness of the 678 is a legitimate scored criterion (CalHR sample matrix row).
15. **"See resume" = experience may simply not be evaluated** (posting boilerplate + CDT + DGS). To/from dates (MM/DD/YY) and hours/week required for every job; without hours the screener can't run the proration.
16. **MQ counting mechanics:** 173.33 hours = one month; screener runs two calculations and credits the greater. Concurrent jobs stack. Duties must be performed routinely, not "occasional or incidental." Paid, unpaid, volunteer, state, and non-state all count equally (SPB Rule 171; CalHR HR Manual 3002).
17. **Self-cert trap (background):** for 24/7 online exams, MQs must be met at self-certification date AND again at appointment; a candidate can be MQ-withheld at hire even after winning the interview (CalHR HR Manual 1105). Not a live risk for us (education-pattern ITS1 via conferred degree; transcript attached).
18. **Stale contact info is a real DQ vector**: departments contact you and your listed supervisors via the 678; unreachable references are one of the most common insider-reported DQ causes (cajobblog + CDFA "reference checks never skipped"). Periodically sanity-check that listed supervisor numbers are still live.
19. **CDT-recommended edge we are not using: state your list rank.** CDT State Hiring Process Guide, Tip 01: if reachable, say so explicitly (e.g. "Reachable on the ITA list") and attach exam results. Rank 1 / score 95 is a differentiator sitting unused.
20. **Spell out acronyms on the 678 too** (SCO official instruction), not just the SOQ.

## 2024-2026 changes that matter

21. **AI boilerplate is now standard on postings**: AI tools "can be helpful in researching responses to the SOQ; however... the SOQ you submit is your own work, in your own words... Submitting an SOQ that is not your own may be cause for disqualification." No statewide AI-detection tooling found; the Oct 2025 Civil Rights Council ADS regulations actually cut against departments running automated AI screening. Enforcement is human judgment. The defense against AI suspicion is dense, verifiable, personal specifics (which generic AI SOQs lack).
22. **Competition is up**: federal-worker pipeline (Work4CA, ~27.7k peak monthly users summer 2025), ~6,002 vacant positions being eliminated through 2026-27 budget, AI-displaced tech workers entering the pool (EO N-6-26, May 2026). Entry IT pools are likely deeper than 2024.
23. **Generalist class consolidation** (67 classes to 8, effective Jan 1, 2026) does not touch the IT series but signals faster, more centralized screening.
24. **ITS I exam (EX-1750) remains a self-rating T&E** as of July 2026: un-timed, online, 100% weighted self-assessment, 70% to pass, 12-month eligibility. Everyone competent scores high, so the exam does not differentiate; the 678 + SOQ carry the real competition.
