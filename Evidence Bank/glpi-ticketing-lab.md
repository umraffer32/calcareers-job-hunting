Self-hosted GLPI (Docker + MariaDB, official `glpi/glpi:11.0.7` image), installed by hand via the web installer, `GLPI_SKIP_AUTOINSTALL=true` so nothing was auto-provisioned. How do you ensure tickets are completed on time? How would you identify problems that may be early indicators of a larger issue?" - 1 page, 12pt, single-spaced). Real configured items, all "Status: done" in the repo:
- **Security hardening.** Rotated all four default GLPI accounts (`glpi` super-admin, `tech`, `normal`, `post-only`) off their publicly-documented default passwords; left the non-interactive `glpi-system` account alone on purpose. Cleared GLPI's own security banner.
- **Incident categorization.** Built a 4-category ITIL tree (Hardware, Software, Network, Access) under Setup -> Dropdowns -> ITIL categories, the key an assignment rule keys off.
- **Ticket triage (the closest thing to "managing tickets").** Logged 2 Incidents and 2 Service Requests, each with independently-set Urgency/Impact/Priority rather than one repeated severity value: a boot-failure Incident (Very High/Medium/High), a partial VPN Incident (Medium/Low), a routine new-monitor Request (Very Low across the board), and an urgent-but-routine password-reset Request (High/Low). NOTE: these are self-authored test tickets in a solo lab, not tickets from real distinct customers - frame honestly as a personal lab demonstrating ticket-workflow mechanics, not job tenure with a live customer queue.
- **SLA + escalation (the direct answer to "how do you ensure tickets are completed on time").** Built `Standard Support SLA` on a 24/7 calendar with a 4-hour "Time to Own" response target and a 2-day "Time to Resolve" resolution target, plus an escalation level (`Nearing Deadline Warning`) that fires 1 day BEFORE the resolution deadline on any still-open ticket and auto-bumps Priority to Very High. Chose a priority-bump action over an email action specifically because it's verifiable without needing SMTP configured.
- **Routing/queue management (also doubles as the "early indicator of a larger issue" answer).** Two groups (`Service Desk`, `Network Team`); a business rule auto-assigns any ticket categorized Network to the Network Team on creation. Verified firing for real (the open Incident's Assigned To shows Network Team added automatically, not manually set). The categorization+routing pairing is what would make a pattern (e.g., a cluster of Network-category tickets) visible as a trend instead of buried in a flat queue - this is the honest hook for the "early indicator of a larger issue" sub-question, NOT a claim that he has done real trend/pattern analysis on a live queue.
- **Asset inventory.** 2 computers, 2 monitors, 1 software entry (Tailscale), tied to tickets for context.
- **Knowledge base.** One article (SSH key-based auth, disabling password login) to cut repeat tickets.
- **Skipped, marked honestly.** Email notifications on ticket update - not configured, would need real SMTP.
