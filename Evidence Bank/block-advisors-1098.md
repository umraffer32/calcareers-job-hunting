Richer version of the difficult-customer story Uriah uses for customer-service SOQ prompts, confirmed by him 2026-06-19. Key added context: as a tax preparer, **whatever a client discloses in the interview cannot legally be ignored** once said. The preparer is obligated to act on it. With this older client, she mentioned she lived alone in her own home. Uriah asked whether she rented or owned. She deflected ("I'm just staying there, don't worry about that"), but because of the legal duty he couldn't drop it. He explained that if she owned the home she would have been issued a 1098 mortgage interest statement, which they needed to file accurately. She reluctantly admitted she owned it and started looking for it on her bank's app. She never produced it. Uriah genuinely doesn't know whether she truly couldn't find it, was stalling to spite him, or just wanted a different preparer. She decided she didn't like him and at one point told him he "has hate in his heart." He didn't take the bait, stayed calm and factual, told her it was fine to work with another preparer with no hard feelings, and handed her off without a fight. The next preparer gave the same requirement for the same reason, she accepted it, and the return was completed correctly. The point the story makes is staying professional and honest with someone who doesn't want your help and takes it personally, and caring about the correct outcome over being liked. Used in the JC-522094 SOQ Q1, but the **"hate in my heart" line was deliberately CUT from the SOQ** (Uriah's call, 2026-06-19). His reasoning: a reader could wonder why she'd say that and assume he did or said something to provoke it, which is an ambiguity that works against him on a scored doc. Keep the line as interview-prep / spoken background only, where he can tell it with context and it shows composure. Default for difficult-customer SOQ answers going forward is to leave out a client's personal insult and let the graceful hand-off carry the professionalism.
