Confirmed by Uriah 2026-07-10, surfaced right after the condo Wi-Fi story below while continuing to tailor the JC-523947 resume. Second real, distinct home-networking project, not a lab. His own home runs a two-unit Eero mesh system, main router downstairs and a satellite router upstairs. The satellite was on wireless backhaul (the two Eero units talking to each other over Wi-Fi instead of a wired link), and upstairs devices saw unreliable, drop-in-and-out connectivity as a result. Fix: bought a 150-foot Cat 6 cable, ran it through the wall from downstairs up to the master bedroom to link the two Eero units over a wired connection instead of wireless backhaul, and added a switch on that run so the printer, TV, and laptop upstairs could connect over full wired Ethernet instead of Wi-Fi. Result was materially better reliability for every device upstairs, both the Eero-served Wi-Fi (now backed by wired backhaul instead of contending for airtime) and the directly wired devices. Real physical cabling work (running structured cable through walls) plus a correct architectural call (wired backhaul beats wireless backhaul for a mesh system), which is strong material for "installing, configuring, and maintaining network hardware" duty language specifically, distinct from the condo story's RF/signal-optimization angle.
