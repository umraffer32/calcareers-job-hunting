# Interview Prep — JC-522622, Service Desk Technician (ITA)

**Department of Consumer Affairs / Board of Vocational Nursing and Psychiatric Technicians (BVNPT)** — Office of Information Services, Client IT Support Services. Sacramento (Natomas). Permanent full-time, multiple positions. Submitted June 25, 2026. No SOQ panel beyond the customer-service prompt already filed; this preps the live interview.

The role is ~half end-user desktop/service-desk support and ~half off-lane work (statistics/data compilation, equipment inventory). The package competed honestly on the support half plus customer service. Below is where a panel will press and the strongest ground to stand on.

## Most likely probe points (the real gaps)

**1. No real production service-desk / ticket-queue tenure.**
This is a Service Desk role and the filed SOQ leaned on coursework labs and internship documentation, not a staffed queue. Expect "What ticketing system have you used? What was your average ticket volume? Walk me through how you manage a queue."
- Honest answer: be upfront that his hands-on ticket work is WGU lab trouble tickets (worked end to end, root-cause, documented) plus logging and tracking remediations during the internship, not yet a production desk seat. Pivot to what tickets actually contain and he does do: troubleshoot to root cause, document the fix so it's repeatable, Active Directory account administration (provisioning, group membership, password resets) from the homelab. Degree plus four CompTIA certs plus fast ramp.
- He is actively building a self-hosted GLPI ITSM ticketing lab to close exactly this gap. Mention it as in-progress learning, do NOT claim it as finished experience.

**2. Hands-on end-user hardware / desktop support (printers, peripherals, mobile devices, copiers) — 25% of the role.**
The internship was fully remote, so he has not done end-user hardware support on a job. Expect "Tell me about supporting a user whose printer or laptop won't work" or "your hands-on hardware experience."
- Honest answer: A+ certified; in his homelab he sets up server hardware and storage, installs operating systems, and troubleshoots his own machines, so the hardware fundamentals are solid. Frame as transferable and fast to ramp on end-user devices, not as prior help-desk hardware tenure. Do not claim imaging (he installs from ISOs, has never captured/deployed an image).

**3. The off-lane half he did not claim: Statistics / data compilation (20%) and Procurement / Equipment Inventory (15%).**
The duty statement includes compiling statistical reports out of BreEZe / Access / Excel and maintaining an equipment/asset inventory database. He has no direct asset-tracking or legislative-reporting experience. Expect a direct "What's your experience compiling reports or managing IT inventory?"
- Honest answer: no direct experience with asset-inventory systems or statistical reporting yet; comfortable with Excel and detail-oriented documentation, organized, and willing to learn the BreEZe and reporting side quickly. Do not overstate. This is the genuine half-the-job gap; own it plainly and lean on learnability.

**4. (Minor) Microsoft 365 admin and macOS** are listed desirables he lacks. If asked, be honest he works in Windows and Linux and has not administered M365 or supported macOS, and would ramp.

## Strongest talking points (lead with these)

**1. Customer service is the real edge.** This role weights customer service heavily and that is his strongest, most genuine ground.
- Block Advisors 1098 client: stayed professional and factual with a hostile customer who did not want his help, handed her off gracefully, and the return still got done right. Composure and caring about the outcome over being liked.
- Helped his neighbor Jason (non-technical) diagnose why a faster internet plan was not delivering speed, explained it in plain terms, and replaced the limiting hardware. Same instinct applied to a tech problem.
- Robert's Retirement: covered a coworker's caseload for a week and coordinated with the partner team so clients were not left waiting. Dependability.

**2. Root-cause troubleshooting, not symptom-patching.**
- WGU lab: half a department dropping off the network intermittently, traced to an exhausted DHCP scope, fixed the cause by shortening the lease time so stale addresses recycled. Also the rogue DNS forward-lookup zone he found and removed.
- Internship: diagnosed and resolved Windows and Linux issues to root cause, ran authenticated Tenable scans, remediated with PowerShell, took one host from 30 findings to 3.

**3. Credentials and systems breadth signal capability and fast ramp.**
- B.S. Network Engineering and Security (WGU, Oct 2025) plus CompTIA A+, Network+, Security+, Cloud+.
- Built a Windows Server 2022 Active Directory domain from scratch in his homelab (two domain controllers, DNS, DHCP, DHCP failover), so he understands the systems behind the desk, not just the front of it.

## Watch-out: the "overqualified / will you stay" read
A security/network grad applying to a desktop-support seat can prompt "are you going to be content here or leave for security?" Be ready to express genuine interest in the hands-on support and customer-facing work, the value of getting in at the ITA level, and the stability of state employment. Frame the security/cloud depth as something he brings TO the desk, not something he is waiting to leave for.
