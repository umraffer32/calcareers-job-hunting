## The landmines (most likely probes)

### 1. "Tell me about your experience on a live service desk with real customers." (THE big one)
This is the gap the whole SOQ danced around: your hands-on ticketing is a **self-built GLPI lab** (you wrote the test tickets yourself) plus WGU lab tickets — no production queue with real customers yet. Don't hide it; frame it well.

**How to answer:**
- Lead with the real, hands-on thing: you built and configured a self-hosted **GLPI ITSM** instance end to end — incident and service-request workflow, four-category triage setting urgency/impact/priority per ticket, an SLA with an escalation rule that flags a ticket a day before its deadline, and an auto-routing rule to the right team. You did that specifically to learn ticket-queue mechanics.
- Back it with the **WGU trouble-ticket labs**: real user-facing incidents worked to root cause (DNS, DHCP, routing, account lockouts, workstation/IP misconfigs).
- Bridge to real people with **Block Advisors**: you carried a caseload of ~70 distinct clients under hard deadlines — real practice juggling many people's problems and prioritizing what's closest to due.
- Close with the honest ramp: "I haven't worked a production desk yet, but I built the queue from scratch to understand it, so I'll be productive on Cherwell or a similar system quickly."
- **Do not** imply the lab was a paid/production desk. Owning that it's a lab reads as confident, not weak.

### 2. "Have you used Cherwell / Microsoft Endpoint Manager / Microsoft 365 Admin?" (named desirables you lack)
**How to answer — honest + transferable, never fake it:**
- **Cherwell:** "Not Cherwell specifically, but I built a real GLPI ITSM instance — incidents, service requests, SLAs, escalation, routing — so the concepts map directly and I'd ramp fast."
- **Endpoint Manager / Intune and M365 Admin:** "I haven't administered those. I have the Windows, Active Directory, and endpoint fundamentals under them, and I pick up tools fast." Then stop — don't stretch it into fake experience.

### 3. The Robert's Retirement "workplace conflict" separation
It's the one negative on the 678 (reason-for-leaving, with Q2 marked No). A panel or background check may ask.

**How to answer:** Keep it short, calm, and forward-looking. It was an interpersonal/workplace conflict, not a performance or misconduct issue; you left professionally, and it was back in 2020. One or two sentences, then pivot to what you've built since (the degree, the CompTIA certs, the internship). **Do not** badmouth the employer or over-explain — brevity reads as composure.

### 4. The 2020–2022 gap (secondary)
Between Robert's (Sep 2020) and Block Advisors (Nov 2022).

**How to answer:** "During COVID I traded options independently and did well at it, but I wanted a stable, building career rather than something that swings year to year, so I went back to school for network engineering and security." Deliberate beats drifting. **Do not** volunteer dollar figures or any setback.

### 5. The lead / analysis / procurement duties (secondary — you're applying up here)
The role is 35% "Analysis" (project lead for analytical studies, process improvement) and 20% procurement/asset inventory.

**How to answer:** Be honest you haven't led formal analytical studies or done state procurement, but point to the adjacent real work — documenting fixes so the next person can follow them, building a knowledge base article to cut repeat tickets, running an asset inventory in the GLPI lab, and process-improvement thinking from the internship. Frame it as ready to grow into, with the support fundamentals already solid.
