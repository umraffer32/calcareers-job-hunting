## Interview prep format — beats, then one flow-through example, never a verbatim script (settled 2026-07-16)

Confirmed by Uriah while prepping JC-520957 for the 2026-07-23 interview; applies to any future interview prep, not just this posting. Build each anticipated question as (1) a short beat list, the elements/evidence to combine, each with a one-line rationale for why it's there, then (2) ONE example of how those beats can flow into a spoken answer, explicitly framed as the shape, not the script. He wants to internalize a flow he can rebuild on the fly under different exact wording, not recall memorized text, since verbatim recall is what breaks under real panel pressure. The deliverable is a separate `Potential_Interview_Questions_and_Answers_JC-######.md` document in the SUBMITTED folder, a companion to `Interview_Prep_JC-######.md` (which stays the source-material, landmine, and logistics reference). Build both once an interview is actually scheduled, per the just-in-time policy above, not before.

Also settled the same session: when a posting's interview is confirmed as a FIXED panel format (the same question set asked to every candidate, no individualized follow-ups, e.g. JC-520957 at roughly 15-20 candidates and 6-7 questions each), build the FULL plausible question pool pulled from the duty statement, the submitted SOQ, and general panel-behavior research, rather than narrowing to a guessed subset of 6-7. Narrowing trades real coverage for a neater prep sheet and risks leaving him unprepared for the one question that got cut. Uriah's explicit call.

**Refined 2026-07-17 (JC-520957 Q7):** beats must be explicitly STAR-labeled (Situation, Task, Action, Result), not a generic topic list. Uriah's words: "what's the first thing that we need to do, what would be the task, what actions would we take, what would the results be." A generic-beats answer for a question with no real single-incident story to anchor it came out as a string of disclaimers with no narrative arc, and he correctly called it weak to read. Fix: anchor every beats list on ONE real situation, even a modest one, and label each beat S/T/A/R. This applies to behavioral and process questions ("tell us about a time," "how do you approach X"); it does not force onto pure knowledge or self-assessment questions (e.g. HIPAA compliance, leading/mentoring) where no story is being asked for, those stay a direct answer plus honest scope. When a question wants a story and no real single-incident one exists, say so plainly rather than force a fabricated STAR arc, honesty still overrides structure.

**Also refined 2026-07-17:** in a scored, non-interactive fixed panel, duty-statement keyword mirroring (already required on the SOQ and 678) applies to spoken answers too, since panelists score only what's said aloud, not what they infer. This matters most for a near-universal, non-duty-statement-sourced question like "why are you the most qualified" (see the JC-520957 Q2 v2 rework), work the duty statement's own verbs into existing true facts through better word choice, not new facts, rather than leaving a strong answer under-scored because it proved depth without touching the rubric's vocabulary.

**Refined 2026-07-20 (JC-520957 Q2 v3):** when a qualifications/proof-heavy answer is drafted as multiple structural variants for Uriah to compare, a proof-first order, open with the strongest project evidence, loop back to credentials as the baseline underneath, close with the CIA triad plus the honest gap line, read smoother to him than a credentials-first order carrying the identical facts. Default to drafting proof-first for this class of question going forward. One data point so far, watch whether it holds on other postings before treating it as fully settled.

**2026-07-23 (JC-520957 actual panel questions, first real ground truth for this method) — a fixed panel can lean heavily on bare definitional knowledge checks, a question type the duty-statement sweep never generated and the beats-plus-flow-through method barely covers.** Uriah's post-interview recall named four questions: what services enterprise companies offer, what is Active Directory, what does a Server Operations Specialist do, and what is redundancy. Only the third matched heavily prepped material (the SOQ-derived responsibilities question, Tier 1 in the doc). The other three were "what is X" style, a phrasing that never once appeared across the 59-question Reddit sweep built for this posting, every one of those 59 was phrased as an experience or behavioral question ("describe your experience," "how do you," "tell us about a time"). Meanwhile "tell me about yourself" and "why are you the most qualified," the two questions research flagged as near-universal and the two the prep process invested the most drafting time in (three structural variants for the qualifications question alone), were not asked at all. **How to apply:** for a fixed-panel interview, run a short plain-English glossary pass alongside the STAR-behavioral beats prep, one or two sentences defining every core named term in the duty statement and posting (Active Directory, redundancy, high availability versus disaster recovery, RAID, virtualization, DNS/DHCP, patch management). Don't leave definitional questions as a low-tier afterthought the way JC-520957's prep did. One real panel so far, watch whether the pattern holds before treating the glossary pass as mandatory for every posting.

**2026-07-23, same day, second catch (Uriah, driving home) — a scored keyword sitting at the end of a memorized list is exactly what drops under live pressure.** In the one question confirmed asked and matched to prepped material (Q13, "what does a Server Operations Specialist do"), the rehearsed daily-duties list ran eight items deep, design, install and configure, maintain and update, monitor, troubleshoot to root cause, patch, track end of life and plan replacement, document and communicate, with documentation last. The keyword was prepped, it just sat at position 8 of 8 in a list recited from memory under real pressure. **How to apply:** when a rehearsed answer is list-structured and more than one item is a literal duty-statement or posting keyword, don't tail-load the highest-value one. Put the two or three keywords that matter most for scoring near the front, or land the single most important one at both the start and the end, rather than trusting a long list to come out in full from memory.

## ⛔ HARD WRITING RULE — NO MID-SENTENCE COLONS (do not violate, ever)

Uriah has flagged this 4-5 times and is rightly fed up. **Never use a colon mid-sentence** before a single explanatory word, clause, or example, in ANYTHING — SOQ, STD 678, resume, cover letter, AND chat messages to him. A colon may ONLY introduce a real list or formatted series. If you are about to write "X: Y" where Y explains or expands X, STOP and rewrite with a period or comma. Example of the violation he hates: "I administer Active Directory directly: creating users and groups." Fix: "I administer Active Directory directly. I create users and groups." Before sending any draft or message, scan every colon and confirm each one introduces a list, not an explanation. This is a zero-tolerance item.

**SOQ is stricter still (settled 2026-06-21): an SOQ contains ZERO colons of any kind**, not even a list-introducing one, because SOQ answers flow as one paragraph with no formatted lists for a colon to introduce. The only exception is a true numeric colon (a clock time like 7:00, a ratio like 3:1). This does NOT extend to the resume, which keeps its label-colons (skills rows, project headers), or to the STD 678. The `tools/verify_doc.py` pre-submit gate enforces the SOQ zero-colon rule and no longer runs the colon check on resumes. One known edge: if a posting's SOQ question text itself contains a colon and you restate the question verbatim (required), the gate will flag that colon. That is an expected false positive on the agency's own wording. Keep the verbatim question and override the flag.

### 2026-07-23 — Some duty statements omit tools by design ("intentional ambiguity"), so a tool-free duty statement means UNKNOWN saturation, not low saturation
Learned by Uriah at the JC-520957 Server Operations Specialist panel on 2026-07-23 and relayed 2026-07-31. He asked the panel why the duty statement named no specific tools or platforms. They told him some postings are written with deliberate "intentional ambiguity," and that when he asked directly what the unit actually runs, the answer was the typical Microsoft stack. One data point from one hiring unit, not a statewide rule, but it undercuts an assumption the triage method makes.

**Why it matters:** the `recommend-job` tool-saturation test measures what share of essential functions depend on a NAMED tool Uriah has zero hands-on with. A duty statement that names nothing therefore scores as low-saturation and reads as a clean fit, purely because there was nothing to count. Several past picks were credited for exactly this (e.g. the 7/23 run praised JC-525673 for having "no named tool anywhere to gate on"). If the omission is deliberate, that scoring is backwards — the tools are still there, just unlisted, and "typical Microsoft stack" is the worst case for him specifically, since Office 365, SharePoint, Exchange, Intune/SCCM, and MDM are all hard gaps.

**How to apply:** when a duty statement names no tools, do NOT score it as low saturation. Score it as UNKNOWN and say so plainly in the recommendation, rather than letting the silence read as a clean bill of health. The desirable-qualifications block and the department's other postings are the best written tells for the real stack. The hiring unit contact on the posting can also simply be asked what the unit runs, which is exactly how Uriah got the answer here, and that call is worth making before investing in an SOQ for any tool-free duty statement. This does not downgrade a genuine fit on its own; it converts a false positive into an open question.
